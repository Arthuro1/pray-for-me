## Purpose
A real Bible-study journey (not 42 disconnected devotionals) showing how the
Psalms give God's people language for praise, trust, fear, grief, anger,
repentance, justice, thanksgiving, waiting and worship.

## Study structure per day (mode 'study')
reflection (framing), study.context (superscription, genre — hymn, lament, royal,
wisdom, pilgrimage/ascent, penitential, imprecatory; historical setting where known;
Hebrew poetry features like parallelism; canonical placement — five books,
Psalm 1–2 as gateway, doxologies; genuine NT use), study.tension (what not to
generalize; misreadings), study.questions (3), study.synthesis (a note / a
practical response), study.prayer (short prayer following the psalm's movement),
related (≤3), resourceTopics.

Across the plan teach readers to ask (spread these as the question stems):
1. What does this Psalm reveal about God? 2. What is the psalmist honestly
experiencing? 3. What movement occurs within the Psalm? 4. What should not be
generalized into a universal promise? 5. How does this Psalm relate to the wider
biblical story? 6. How can its language be faithfully turned into prayer?
Make day 1 teach how to study a psalm (the six questions) and week boundaries
(days 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42) include a brief review question in synthesis.

## Guardrails
- Handle imprecatory psalms carefully (69, maybe 137 as related, 109): honest
  anger handed to God rather than taken into our hands; Jesus' teaching on enemies;
  no license for curses or revenge; justice belongs to God.
- Do not erase lament by forcing instant emotional resolution — Psalm 88 ends in
  darkness; say so and let it stand.
- Do not claim every Psalm is directly spoken by Christ; note genuine NT use
  (Psalm 2, 16, 22, 110, 118, 69, 8 in Hebrews 2).
- Psalm 91: not a guarantee against all harm (Satan misuses it in Matthew 4:6).
- Psalm 37 / 1: not a prosperity guarantee; Psalm 73 is the counterweight.
- Superscriptions: note they're ancient but debated as to authorship/setting.
- safetyNote on despair-heavy days (22, 69, 77, 88; also 13, 42 if useful): if
  you feel hopeless or think about ending your life, contact emergency services
  or a crisis line and tell someone you trust now.
