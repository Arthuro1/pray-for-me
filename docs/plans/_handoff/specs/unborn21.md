## Purpose
A gentle, Scripture-centred prayer journey during pregnancy: praying for the
developing child, the mother, the father/other parent and family, caregivers, the
home, the future — and ultimately entrusting everything to God. THIS PLAN NEEDS
ESPECIALLY CAREFUL PASTORAL AND SAFETY WORDING.

## Never imply
- that prayer guarantees a healthy pregnancy or a particular birth outcome;
- that pregnancy loss results from insufficient faith or sin;
- that medical care is unnecessary;
- that parents failed spiritually if complications occur.
Prayer explicitly coexists with prenatal and medical care — say so in the intro
and on the relevant days (e.g. "keep every appointment", "tell your midwife or
doctor about…"). Pregnancy loss, infertility, trauma and serious medical content
are sensitive.

## Completion wording
Must work regardless of pregnancy stage AND regardless of outcome (the reader may
finish before birth, after birth, or after a loss). The completion must not
assume a live birth.

## Themes
thanksgiving for life; God knows this child; development in the womb; the
mother's health and strength; peace amid fear; the father/other parent; siblings;
grandparents; medical professionals; provision; the home the child may enter;
parenting wisdom; future faith; character; Christian community; protection;
delivery; postpartum season (incl. mental health — gentle line to seek help for
low mood/anxiety after birth); surrender under uncertainty.

## Passages (read carefully)
Psalm 139:13-18, Psalm 22:9-10, Psalm 121, Psalm 127, Luke 1 (Mary and Elizabeth
— descriptive, not a template), James 1:5, Philippians 4:6-7, Isaiah 40:11 (He
gently leads those that have young), Jeremiah 1:4-5 WITHOUT generalizing
Jeremiah's unique prophetic calling to every child (say so), Isaiah 49:15-16,
Psalm 71:6, Genesis 1:27-28, 1 Samuel 1–2 (Hannah — careful: descriptive),
Matthew 6:25-34, Romans 8:28 (careful — not a promise that all ends well).

## Safety notes (use `safetyNote`)
- Early in the plan (day 1 or 2): if you have bleeding, severe pain, reduced
  movements or any worrying symptom, contact your midwife/doctor or emergency
  services now — prayer and medical care belong together.
- A day on fear/uncertainty: if you have experienced loss before, or this
  pregnancy ends in loss, this is not your fault or a failure of faith; name
  pastoral care and bereavement support.
- Postpartum: low mood, anxiety or thoughts of harming yourself or the baby — tell
  your doctor/midwife or emergency services immediately.
- If you are not safe at home (domestic abuse in pregnancy) — seek help.
Keep them calm, brief, non-alarmist.
