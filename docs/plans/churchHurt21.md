**Status:** Drafted with AI assistance on 2026-09-23. Not reviewed by a human.
EN and FR authored; the other 14 languages show AI-drafted day titles and fall

## 1. Purpose and audience

For readers carrying wounds connected to a church, a ministry environment or a
Christian leader. The range is wide on purpose: disappointment and conflict at
one end, spiritual, emotional, sexual or financial abuse at the other. The plan
helps the reader lament honestly, distinguish Christ from the people who failed
them, understand forgiveness, justice and boundaries, and — only if and when it
is safe — reconsider Christian community. About ten minutes a day. The reader
may be both hurt and hurting, or a leader who caused harm; day 15 names that
once, without moving the focus off the wounded.

## 3. Passages considered and rejected

- **Hebrews 10:25** — not a primary text. It appears once, as a related passage
  on day 19, framed as an invitation to encouragement "not a rule to be used
  against the wounded", with explicit permission to take time and choose a safe
  community. A test pins that wording and forbids any other day from citing it.
- **Matthew 18:15-17** — used only as related on day 14, under Matthew 18:1-6
  (the little ones). The text says the steps suit ordinary offences between
  people who can speak as equals and never require anyone to confront an abuser
  alone; criminal conduct must not be concealed under church discipline.
- **Hebrews 13:17** — related on day 17 only, with its limit stated: it assumes
  accountable leaders who care for souls and never asks anyone to submit to abuse.
- **Psalm 105:15 / 1 Chronicles 16:22** ("do not touch my anointed") — the verse
  most often used to shield leaders. In context it protects the vulnerable
  patriarchs *from* powerful kings. Left out so the slogan is never put in front
  of a wounded reader; a reviewer may want a day that corrects it.
- **1 Samuel 24 and 26 (David and Saul)** — David spares Saul yet does not return
  when Saul promises safety (1 Samuel 26:21-25; 27:1). Strong for "forgiveness
  without access", but its "the Lord's anointed" frame risks reinforcing
  deference to leaders. Offered as an alternative for day 12.
- **1 Peter 2:18-25** — rejected: historically used to keep victims in abuse.
  Only 1 Peter 2:25 (the Shepherd and Overseer of souls) is cited, on day 21.
- **Matthew 18:21-35** (the unforgiving servant) — rejected: easily used to
  pressure victims to forgive on demand.
- **Romans 13:1-2** — deliberately not cited; only 13:3-4 (authorities acting
  against wrongdoing), as the spec asks. 13:1-2 is often misapplied to church
  leaders.
- **Genesis 42–45 / 50 (Joseph testing his brothers)** — considered for "trust is
  rebuilt slowly"; rejected as narrative easily over-read. 2 Timothy 4:14-18 makes
  the same point more directly.

## 4. Interpretive issues and how the text handles them

- **Scope of Matthew 18:15-17.** Safeguarding bodies (e.g. thirtyone:eight,
  GRACE) and many pastors say it covers peer offences, not abuse with a power
  imbalance or crime; others treat it as a universal procedure. The text takes
  the first view and says so plainly (day 14). Theology reviewer: confirm.
- **Romans 13:3-4 applied to reporting.** Day 13 reads Paul's ban on revenge
  together with the authorities' God-given role against wrongdoing: refusing
  revenge is not refusing justice. It does not claim every authority is just.
- **Is forgiveness conditional on repentance?** (cf. Luke 17:3-4.) Evangelicals
  differ. The plan does not settle it: forgiveness is described as refusing
  revenge and entrusting the debt to God, often a process, and always distinct
  from trust, access and reconciliation (days 11–13, 15).
- **2 Timothy 4:14** — some manuscripts and translations read a wish ("may the
  Lord repay him"). The paraphrase "leaves repayment to the Lord" fits either.
- **2 Corinthians 7:11** — the NIV has "readiness to see justice done"; ESV
  "punishment", LSG « punition ». Day 15 paraphrases it as eagerness "to put
  things right and to see justice done" / « de voir la justice rendue ». Check.
- **Luke 19:8** — a minority reading takes Zacchaeus' words as a description of
  his existing practice rather than a vow. The plan follows the majority reading.
- **Anointing language (day 10).** Written for a Pentecostal/charismatic
  audience: it affirms the Spirit's anointing on Jesus and says its purpose was
  release, not domination, so that "anointing" is not left as a tool of control.
- **Revelation 7:13-17 (day 21)** is used as future hope, explicitly not as a
  promise of present healing ("does not erase what happened or hurry you now").

## 5. Safety concerns and how the text handles them

Safety notes (all tested to name real help): **1, 5, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 19, 21.**

- **Day 1** — immediate danger → emergency services or police; abuse → independent
  safeguarding body, counsellor, medical professional; abuse of a child or a
  vulnerable adult must be reported, without waiting for church permission.
- **Day 5** — threats/coercion need help independent of the person's authority;
  do not meet the person alone.
- **Days 11–12 (forgiveness)** — forgiveness never requires silence, contact or
  staying unsafe; pressure to forgive quickly or drop a complaint → independent
  counsellor, safeguarding professional, pastor outside the situation; you may
  decline contact; stalking/threats → keep a record, police, victim support,
  legal adviser.
- **Days 13–14 (justice/reporting)** — reporting abuse is not unforgiveness;
  crimes to the police; child/vulnerable-adult concerns reported even if leaders
  prefer to handle it internally; no need to meet the person first; sexual
  abuse, violence, threats and financial exploitation can go straight to police.
- **Day 15 (repentance)** — no obligation to meet to hear an apology; amends from
  a leader should go through an independent process; a reader who has abused
  someone is pointed to a pastor, a safeguarding professional or a lawyer.
- **Day 19 (community)** — never required to return where you were abused, where
  the person still has influence, or where concerns were covered up; ask about a
  safeguarding policy; take a friend; a counsellor can help with pacing.
- **Day 21** — recovery often takes longer than a plan; keep seeking support.

Other choices: every practice is optional and none asks the reader to contact
the person who harmed them; writing practices say "somewhere safe and private"
(days 2, 9); day 1 says the plan can be paused at any time; the mild end is
served explicitly (day 1, day 11 "after a small hurt…", day 13 practice).

## 7. What the reviewers must check

Theology reviewer:
- Day 14's reading of Matthew 18:15-17 (peer offences, not abuse).
- Day 13's use of Romans 13:3-4 for reporting; day 17's handling of Hebrews 13:17.
- Days 11–12: the description of forgiveness (not denial; distinct from trust,
  access and reconciliation) is faithful and not therapeutic-only.
- Day 6's observation from John 10:5 (sheep that refuse a stranger's voice).
- Day 10's anointing language for a Pentecostal/charismatic audience.
- No prose quotes Scripture; paraphrases (e.g. 2 Corinthians 7:11) are fair.

Safety reviewer:
- Reporting language: "must be reported" (days 1, 13) states a safeguarding
  standard; legal duties vary by country — confirm the wording does not
  overstate or understate them.
- Day 14 practice asks a reader with a concern about a child to contact police or
  protection services "today" — confirm tone and urgency.
- Written-record practices (days 2, 9, 13, 14) could be discovered by an abuser
  on a shared device or home — confirm the "safe and private" qualifiers suffice.
- Day 15's word to readers who have abused someone.
- Nothing silences, blames, pressures to reconcile, or frames leaving as rebellion
  (tests guard phrasing, but a human must read for tone).

Native reviewers: all 14 non-EN/FR day titles and the i18n JSON are AI drafts;
FR prose uses **tu** and needs a native read.
