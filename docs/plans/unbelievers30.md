# unbelievers30 — Praying for Those Who Don't Yet Believe

**Status:** Drafted with AI assistance on 2026-09-23 (days 8–30, meta prose, tests and this note completed 2026-09-24 after an interrupted run). Not reviewed by a human. EN and FR authored; the other 14 languages show AI-drafted day titles and fall back to English prose.

Files: `src/content/plans/prayingForUnbelievers.js` (meta), `prayingForUnbelieversDays.js` (days), `prayingForUnbelievers.test.js` (contract + guardrails). UI keys: `scratchpad/i18n/unbelievers30.json` (`planUnbelieversTitle`, `planUnbelieversSub`, `planUnbelieversMovementHeart|Understanding|Witness|Mission`).

## 1. Purpose and audience

A 30-day intercession plan (category `others`, ~10 minutes a day) for believers who want to pray steadily for people who have **not yet** come to faith — a friend, relative, colleague, neighbour — and for the nations. It is also meant to shape the reader into a loving, honest, courageous witness, so every day carries a `selfPrompt` that turns the prayer back on the reader (courage, integrity, listening, repentance for harm Christians have done).

People are never "projects" or "targets", and friendship is not a tactic. People who once believed and have drifted away belong to the separate `prodigal30` plan. The intro points them there without naming it, and a test keeps the word "prodigal" out of the prose.

## 3. Passages considered and rejected

- **All spec passages are used**: Luke 15, Luke 19:10, John 3, John 6:44, Romans 1:16 (related, day 16), Romans 10, 1 Corinthians 2 (related, day 11), 2 Corinthians 4, Ephesians 2, Colossians 4, 1 Timothy 2, 2 Peter 3:9, 1 Peter 3:15-16, Acts 16:14, Acts 17, Matthew 9, Romans 9:1-3, John 4, John 17:20-23, Acts 26:18.
- **Added**: Acts 8 (Philip and the Ethiopian official: understanding Scripture begins with the reader's own question), Acts 4:23-31 (Spirit-given boldness, prayed in community, a natural fit for a Pentecostal/charismatic audience), Mark 5:18-20 with John 7:5 and Acts 1:14 (witness at home; Jesus' own brothers did not believe at first), Acts 13:1-4 (prayer and fasting for those who are sent), Hebrews 13:3 and Acts 12 (the persecuted church; Peter freed and James killed, so prayer is not presented as a guarantee), Luke 18:1-8 and 1 Samuel 12:23 (perseverance), 1 Corinthians 3:5-9 (God gives the growth), Romans 11:33-36 and Genesis 18 (surrender), Revelation 7:9-12 (close).
- **Rejected**: 1 Peter 3:1-2 (a wife "winning" a husband without words). Applied loosely, it can be used to keep someone in a controlling or abusive marriage, so day 20 uses Mark 5 and a safety note instead. Isaiah 55:11 (easily read as a promise that a particular person will respond). Philippians 1:6 (it concerns believers, and applying it to unbelievers reads as a guarantee). Matthew 18:18 / "binding and loosing" (spec forbids binding formulas). Proverbs 11:30 (the wording of the "wins souls" line is textually disputed and invites a scalp-counting frame). Luke 15:11-32 (the lost son belongs to the prodigal plan). Ezekiel 3/33 "watchman" texts (they easily turn into guilt and fear pressure on the one praying).

## 4. Interpretive issues and disagreements

- **Election and human response** (John 6:44; Romans 9–11; 1 Timothy 2:4; 2 Peter 3:9). Reformed readers take the Father's drawing as effectual for the elect and "all/anyone" in 1 Timothy 2:4 / 2 Peter 3:9 as all kinds of people or the elect community. Arminian/Wesleyan readers, which include most Pentecostal traditions, take the drawing as enabling for all and God's desire as universal. The text never picks a side. Day 5 says Christians explain God's desire differently. Day 10 names the disagreement and says the plan "does not settle it". Day 27 notes that "anyone" is read in different ways. Day 29 follows Paul, who ends Romans 9–11 in worship rather than in a system. The practical agreement both sides share is stated: coming to Christ is God's work, so pray, and keep inviting.
- **Spiritual blindness** (2 Corinthians 4:4; Acts 26:18). This is taken seriously as a spiritual reality, but it is prayed "soberly" and "without formulas". There are no commands to spirits and no "binding" of a person's mind (tested). Day 8 connects Paul's answer to blindness with his refusal of deceptive methods.
- **Romans 2:4** is usually quoted about unbelievers, but in context it addresses the judgmental religious person. Day 13 keeps that context and turns it on the reader.
- **Luke 18:1-8** is a parable about justice. The day keeps that, uses Luke's own framing in verse 1 (keep praying, don't lose heart), and stresses the contrast: God is not the unjust judge, and persistence is not leverage.
- **Mark 5:18-20 / John 7:5 / Acts 1:14**: the brothers' later faith is told as "their story, not a promise about your family".
- **Revelation 7:10**: day 30 avoids saying the crowd "does not credit their own choices", which would lean on one side of the election debate. It says only that their song is not about themselves.
- **Hebrews 13:3 / Acts 12**: prayer did not spare James, and that is said plainly.

## 5. Safety concerns and how the text handles them

- **Guaranteed-conversion language**: none. The intro says "no plan can promise anyone's conversion". Day 28 says faithfulness isn't measured by conversions, and days 29–30 hand the outcome to God. The tests block phrasing like "will be saved", "claim their salvation", "God will save" and "because you prayed", along with the French equivalents.
- **Manipulation and pressure**: day 8's self-prompt asks the reader to look for pressure or a hidden agenda in their own witness. Day 12 says the reader need not manufacture guilt. Day 13's practice is kindness "with no spiritual message attached". Day 18 is about answering when asked, with gentleness. Day 19 is about listening "without steering the answer". The tests block fear and pressure idioms.
- **Family who reject the faith (day 20)**: the practice says to honour a relative's request not to raise faith. **Safety note**: respect boundaries; if home is controlling, abusive or unsafe, safety comes first; talk to a pastor, counsellor or trusted person; call emergency services in immediate danger; faithful witness never requires staying in danger.
- **People harmed by Christians (day 21)**: the day confesses rather than defends. **Safety note**: believe the person, don't push them back to church, don't press forgiveness or reconciliation; report to the church's safeguarding lead and to the police where a crime may have been committed; a counsellor can help.
- **Other faiths (day 22)**: the day asks for respect, curiosity, listening to learn rather than to rebut, and prayer for people's families and wellbeing. Caricature words ("pagans", "false religions", "heathens", "cults"…) are blocked by a test.
- **Persecuted church (day 25)**: the day prays for the persecutors, prays for freedom of belief "for people of every faith and none", and the self-prompt guards against us-against-them thinking.
- **Fasting (day 24)**: it is optional and conditioned on health ("If your health allows, skip one meal").

## 7. What reviewers must check

**Theology reviewer**
- Days 5, 10, 27 and 29: is the election material fair to both Reformed and Arminian/Pentecostal readers, and does it stay out of the argument?
- Days 8 and 14: is spiritual blindness / "power of Satan" handled at the right weight for a charismatic audience, being neither dismissive nor formulaic?
- Day 12: the conviction-versus-shame distinction.
- Day 13: Romans 2:4 read in context and applied to the reader.
- Day 16: prayer for healing and "signs" (Acts 4:30) is phrased as a request, not an expectation. Is that right?
- Day 21: identificational confession on the model of Daniel 9. Is it acceptable across the ministry's traditions?
- Day 26: using a justice parable for evangelistic intercession.
- Day 30: the reading of Revelation 7:10.
- Check that no sentence reads as Scripture quotation. Some close paraphrases were kept short on purpose (e.g. "salvation belongs to God and to the Lamb", "the Judge of all the earth does what is right").

**Safety reviewer**
- The day 20 and day 21 safety notes, including whether "safeguarding lead" / "la personne chargée de la prévention des abus" suits every region.
- The day 24 fasting practice.
- The day 21 practice (listening to someone hurt by a church) must not pressure disclosure.
- Days 22 and 25 must not read as hostile toward any faith or country.

**Language reviewers**
- The 16-language day titles and the i18n JSON are AI drafts. Check church vocabulary in `am`, `sw`, `tl`, `fa` and `hi` in particular. Day 12's title (Spirit who convicts) and day 29's title ("fathom") are the most idiomatic-sensitive.

## 8. Outstanding questions

- The intro mentions "a separate plan" for people who once believed. If `prodigal30` ships under a known title, the intro could name it.
- Day 23's practice mentions "a mission prayer guide your church trusts" without naming one. Operation World, Joshua Project and Open Doors are candidates for the resource shelf once reviewed.
- No `unreached-peoples` or `other-faiths` resource topic exists. `mission` and `apologetics` cover them for now. A dedicated `other-faiths` topic (respectful introductions to world religions for Christians) would sharpen day 22's shelf.
- Two small corrections were made to the interrupted run's days 1–7. Day 2 now says Zacchaeus *promised* restitution rather than having already repaid it. Day 4 had wording too close to a Bible translation ("harassed and helpless") and a garbled sentence, both now fixed.
