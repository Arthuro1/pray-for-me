## 1. Purpose and audience

Biblical womanhood as a life submitted to God and increasingly conformed to Christ — **not** a wife-and-mother plan. Written for single, married and widowed women, mothers and women without children, young adults and older women, women working in the home and outside it. It is built on the women of Scripture (one woman or passage per day, read in context), so it stands as its own curriculum beside `manOfGod21`: the only primary passage the two plans share is Luke 7:36-50, read there as "seeing women as Jesus did" and here as the woman's own story (tested).

## 3. Passages considered and rejected

- **Hannah (1 Samuel 1)** — considered for a whole day; set aside so that infertility is not the subject of a general formation plan's day (it would need its own safety note and the sensitive `infertility` tag). Hannah's *song* is honoured on day 19 as the root of Mary's. A reviewer may prefer a Hannah day — see §8.
- **Titus 2:3-5** — its v5 raises the disputed submission question a second time; spiritual mentoring is carried instead by Deborah, Romans 16:13 and 2 Timothy 1:5 (day 18). Not rejected on principle; see §9.
- **1 Timothy 2:8-15 and Ephesians 5:21-33** — not used as primary passages (Ephesians 5 is the Man of God plan's marriage day). Ephesians 5:21 is cited once, fairly, as part of one reading of 1 Peter 3.
- **The Samaritan woman (John 4)**, **Dorcas (Acts 9:36-42)**, **Rahab**, **Elizabeth**, **Mark 14:3-9** — good candidates, cut for length; the witness theme sits on Mary Magdalene (day 21), service on days 15–16.
- **Song of Songs** — avoided per spec.
- **2 Timothy 1:3-7, 2 Timothy 4:6-8** — primary passages of `manOfGod21`; avoided as primaries to keep the plans distinct.

## 4. Interpretive issues and theological disagreements

The owner decision is followed: shared ground only; the disagreement is named, never adjudicated (a test forbids "the correct/true reading" phrasing and "wives/women must submit/obey").

- **Day 12 — 1 Peter 3:1-6 (FLAGGED, the only `marriage-roles` day).** Read in its context (1 Peter 2:11–3:7: believers doing good under rulers, masters and unbelieving husbands, for the sake of witness). The reflection says Christians read the call to submit differently — a lasting pattern under a husband's loving leadership (complementarian) or missional counsel for that culture within mutual submission (egalitarian, citing Ephesians 5:21) — says the plan does not settle it, and keeps to what both share: never sin, never silence about harm, never staying in danger. Peter's "weaker vessel" (3:7) is not quoted or explained; reviewers may want a line on it. The promise that a husband "may be won" is presented as hope, not certainty.
- **Day 2 — ezer** is used most often of God as Israel's help (Psalm 121, 33); the day notes that Christians differ on what Genesis 2 implies for roles.
- **Day 3 — Eve.** Genesis 3:6 notes her husband was with her; Romans 5 traces sin's entrance through Adam. 1 Timothy 2:13-14 is not cited; reviewers may want it acknowledged.
- **Day 14 — Acts 2 / Philip's daughters / 1 Corinthians 12.** Names that Christians differ on how women's gifts relate to some church offices; affirms only that the Spirit gifts women. The Pentecostal emphasis (asking to be filled afresh) promises no particular experience: "as He chooses".
- **Day 15 — Romans 16.** Phoebe's *diakonos* is given as "servant — the word can also be translated deacon". **Junia (v7)** is inside the primary range but not discussed (her name/gender and "outstanding among / well known to the apostles" are both debated); this is a deliberate omission to avoid adjudicating — reviewers may prefer one fair sentence.
- **Day 18 — Deborah.** One sentence says Christians draw different conclusions from her leadership for church life today. "Mother in Israel" (Judges 5:7) is read as care for a people; the text mentions no children (4:4 "wife of Lappidoth" is itself debated).
- **Day 6 — Proverbs 31** is an acrostic poem praising wisdom embodied (the "woman of valour", the phrase Boaz uses of Ruth in Ruth 3:11), not a checklist; its climax is the fear of the LORD (v30).
- **Day 16** — the midwives' reply to Pharaoh (Exodus 1:19) has long been debated as a lie; the day does not discuss it.
- **Day 20** — Luke 2:37 can mean "a widow until she was 84" or "a widow for 84 years"; the day says only "great age".
- Narrative is treated as descriptive throughout (Esther's "who knows" is a question, not a promise; Hagar's return in 16:9 is not a rule).

## 5. Safety concerns and how the text handles them

Safety notes (tested) on days **4, 8, 10, 11, 12**:
- **4 (Hagar)** — being sent back was not a rule that anyone must stay where they are harmed; talk to someone trusted, a pastor; emergency services if in danger.
- **8 (Leah, body image)** — restricting food, bingeing, purging or self-harm: doctor or trusted person; urgent help.
- **10 (sexual holiness)** — sexual coercion or assault is not the victim's sin; counsellor, pastor, police.
- **11 (Naomi, grief)** — prolonged grief and suicidal thoughts: doctor, pastor, local emergency number.
- **12 (1 Peter 3)** — submission never means enduring abuse, threats, coercion or control, or keeping silent about harm; pastor, counsellor, police; "No teaching requires you to stay where you are being harmed."

Other safeguards: day 17's fasting practice excludes pregnancy, illness and eating struggles; day 5 notes that not every illness is healed in this life; no day names infertility or miscarriage; womanhood is never defined by appearance, marriage, motherhood, passivity or male approval, and singleness is never a waiting room (all tested).
