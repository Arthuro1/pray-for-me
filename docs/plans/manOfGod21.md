# manOfGod21 — Man of God: Biblical Identity for Men (21 days)

**Status:** Drafted with AI assistance on 2026-09-23. Not reviewed by a human.
EN and FR authored; the other 14 languages show AI-drafted day titles and fall
back to English prose.

Files: `src/content/plans/manOfGod.js` (meta + guardrail header),
`manOfGodDays.js` (21 days), `manOfGod.test.js` (contract + guardrails). UI
keys: `planManOfGod*` in the scratch i18n JSON `i18n/manOfGod21.json` (not yet
merged into the locales).

## 1. Purpose and audience

Biblical manhood as a life lived before God and slowly conformed to Christ —
**not** a marriage-role plan. Every day is written to be worth praying for a
single man, a married man, a father, a man without children, a young adult, an
older man, a leader and a man with no title. Marriage appears on one day (13)
and fatherhood only as spiritual fatherhood (19), each with an explicit word to
the men it does not describe (Jesus and Paul single; fatherhood in the faith
open "with or without children").

It is a distinct curriculum from `womanOfGod21`, not the same plan with swapped
pronouns: male biblical examples (Adam, Jesus, Peter, Timothy, Joseph, David,
Jonathan, Nehemiah, Daniel, Paul, Joshua) and pressures men commonly name —
proving yourself, performance, hidden emotion, pornography, anger, power,
loneliness, income as worth, the loss of strength with age.

Mode: prayer. Category: `formation`. Shelf domain: `christian-living`.

## 3. Passages

**Used** — all of the spec's list except as noted below, plus: Genesis 2:7-15
(Adam given breath and a garden before any wife, child or reputation; 2:15
"work and keep" — *ʿābad*/*šāmar*, also "serve"/"guard"); Mark 1:9-13 +
Matthew 4:1-4 (sonship before ministry); Luke 5:1-11 (Peter's confession);
John 11:32-44 + Hebrews 5:7 + Psalm 13 (tears); Luke 7:36-50 ("Do you see this
woman?"); Nehemiah 5 (protecting without exploiting); Daniel 6 (a habit of
prayer); 2 Corinthians 12 (strength in weakness); 2 Timothy 4:9-11 with Acts
15:37-39 in prose (Paul asks for Mark).

**Considered and rejected**
- *Genesis 3:16* ("he shall rule over you") — part of the curse, not the
  creation design; putting it in a men's plan without long discussion invites
  misuse. Genesis 1–2 carries the theme instead.
- *1 Timothy 2:8-15; 1 Peter 3:1-7 as primary texts* — the most disputed texts,
  and about women and church order more than about a man's character. 1 Peter
  3:7 is kept only as a related reference on day 13.
- *Judges 13–16 (Samson)* as a lesson on "strength" — too easily turned into
  "women as a snare"; Joseph and David carry the sexual-integrity theme.
- *Proverbs 5–7 (the adulteress)* — the figure of the seductive woman is what
  the owner asked us to avoid; Job 31:1 and 1 Thessalonians 4 carry the theme
  without casting women as temptresses.
- *1 Kings 2:2* ("show yourself a man") — easily read as a masculine ideal, and
  it sits in a deathbed charge that goes on to revenge instructions.
  1 Corinthians 16:13-14 carries courage with love attached.
- *Malachi 4:6; Psalms 127–128* for fatherhood — assume biological children;
  day 19 deliberately uses spiritual fatherhood.

## 4. Interpretive issues and disagreements

- **Day 13 — Ephesians 5:21-33 (owner decision: shared ground only).** The day
  says Christians who love the passage read "head" differently: a husband's
  loving, servant leadership (complementarian) or verse 21's mutual submission
  as the frame for the whole passage (egalitarian). It does **not** adjudicate
  (a test forbids "correct/true/only biblical reading") and keeps to what both
  share: Christlike self-giving love, nourishing and cherishing, never
  harshness (Colossians 3:19), never control. Only day tagged `marriage-roles`.
  **Flagged for theology review.**
- **Day 9 — 1 Timothy 3:1-7 (related).** One clause notes that "Christians
  differ on who may hold that office, but not on the character it describes",
  and uses only the character list (not violent, not quarrelsome). No
  `marriage-roles` tag. **Flag for theology review** under the same owner
  decision.
- **Day 8 — 1 Thessalonians 4:4.** *skeuos* ("vessel") is read as "body" (NIV,
  ESV, most modern versions) or "wife" (RSV, some commentators). The day
  follows "body". Verse 6 ("wrong his brother") is applied broadly: sexual sin
  wrongs another person.
- **Day 10 — Bathsheba.** Nathan's parable places the guilt on the man with
  power, and the day calls Bathsheba "the lamb, not the villain". Some
  commentators argue for her agency; the plan follows the parable's own
  framing (2 Samuel 12:1-9). "Forgiveness came at once; the consequences did
  not disappear" follows 12:13-14 without dwelling on the child's death.
- **Day 11 — David and Jonathan.** Read as covenant friendship between men
  (1 Samuel 18:3; 20:41-42; 23:16), the historic mainstream reading; modern
  romantic readings are not engaged. Kissing and weeping are presented as
  ordinary signs of deep friendship in that culture.
- **Day 17 — 1 Corinthians 16:13.** *andrizesthe* is literally "act like men",
  but it is addressed to the whole church and tied to love in verse 14; the day
  says so, so courage is not made a male-only virtue.
- **Paul's marital status.** Day 13 says Paul "was single when he wrote" (not
  "never married" — some hold he was a widower); day 19 says "as far as we
  know" he had no children.
- **Day 21 — the crown.** Kept to the text (a crown from the Lord, the
  righteous Judge, for all who long for Christ's appearing), with no
  merit / anti-merit gloss.
- **Day 1 — "rule".** Genesis 1:28's dominion is given to male and female
  together and framed as stewardship, never domination.

## 5. Safety

Safety notes are calm, name real help and never shame (all tested):
- **Day 4** (emotional honesty) — heaviness for weeks or thoughts of suicide:
  tell a doctor, pastor or trusted friend today; emergency number if in
  immediate danger.
- **Day 8** (sexuality and pornography) — "not beyond help", "shame is not the
  way out"; pastor, mature believer, qualified counsellor, accountability
  software; anything involving a minor or someone who did not consent: stop and
  seek professional help immediately.
- **Day 9** (anger) — if your anger has frightened, threatened or hurt someone,
  or you fear it might: pastor or counsellor this week, a programme that helps
  men stop violent behaviour; emergency number if anyone is in danger now.
- **Day 10** (abuse of power) — repentance includes stopping, getting help and
  accepting accountability, including before the authorities where that
  applies; do not contact the person harmed in any way that pressures them.
- **Day 13** (Ephesians 5) — the passage never licenses control, threats or
  violence; if anyone at home is afraid of you, or you are afraid: pastor or
  counsellor; emergency number if someone is in danger.

Treatment of women: consent and respect ("respecting her no", day 12); "someone
made in God's image and never an object" (day 8); Joseph pressed by "someone
with power over him", not a temptress (day 7). Tests also rule out "real men",
"men don't cry", "man up", domination language, violence as strength, wealth as
manhood, marriage or fatherhood as a condition of manhood, and shaming words.

## 7. What the reviewers must check

**Theology**
- Day 13 (Ephesians 5) and the day 9 clause on 1 Timothy 3: does the wording
  meet the owner's "shared ground only" decision, fairly to both readings?
- Day 1: the "serve/guard" gloss on Genesis 2:15 and the stewardship framing of
  Genesis 1:28.
- Day 8: the "body" reading of 1 Thessalonians 4:4.
- Day 10: Bathsheba as "the lamb, not the villain".
- Day 17: *andrizesthe* presented as a charge to the whole church.
- Day 19: spiritual fatherhood "carries a mother's gentleness as well as a
  father's urging" (1 Thessalonians 2:7-12).
- Whether the Munroe and Poonen `marriage-roles` titles should appear outside
  day 13 on this plan (§6).

**Safety**
- The five notes (days 4, 8, 9, 10, 13) — in particular: does day 10 need an
  explicit emergency line, and is naming "accountability software" generically
  acceptable (day 8)?
- Day 12: prompts about women being harassed or controlled — do they point to
  safe action rather than a man stepping in alone?
- Day 20 mentions depression without a note (day 4 carries the main one) — is
  that enough?

title languages.
