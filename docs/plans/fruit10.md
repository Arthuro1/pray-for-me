## 1. Purpose and audience

For any believer who wants to grow in Christlike character. The plan treats
character as **fruit** that the Spirit produces in people joined to Christ.
It is not a self-improvement checklist. Day 1 reads Galatians 5:13-26 in
context (freedom used to serve in love, flesh against Spirit). Days 2–10 take
the nine qualities in Paul's order, each from a passage read in its own
setting. Every day runs the same rhythm: receive (reflection), examine
(`selfPrompt`, on every day, honest and never shaming), pray (3 prompts) and
practise (one concrete action). A day takes about 10–15 minutes.

## 2. Structure

The three movements group the nine qualities as "life in the Spirit / toward
others / steady character". **This triad grouping is a traditional teaching
aid, not Paul's own structure.** Galatians 5:22-23 is one list of one fruit, and
the plan says so (biblical note, day 1).

## 3. Passages considered and rejected

- **Galatians 5:22-23 as the ref every day.** Rejected: it would make the plan
  a word study of nine nouns. Each quality gets a passage where it is embodied
  or explained in context. Galatians still opens (day 1), returns (day 7,
  6:7-10; day 9, 6:1-2) and closes (day 10, 5:24-25).
- **Day 2 (love): 1 John 4 as the primary.** Kept as related. 1 Corinthians 13
  was chosen because its context is a gifted, charismatic church that lacked
  love (chapters 12–14). That speaks directly to a Pentecostal readership.
- **Day 3 (joy): Philippians 4:4 or Nehemiah 8:10 alone.** Rejected as the
  primary. "The joy of the LORD is your strength" is often pulled out of its
  setting: in Nehemiah 8:9-12 the people are weeping at the law, and the
  feast includes sending portions to those with nothing. Kept as related, and
  read in context. The primary is John 15:9-11, spoken the night of the betrayal.
- **Day 4 (peace): Philippians 4:6-7 alone.** Widened to 4:2-9 so the passage
  includes the Euodia–Syntyche conflict. Peace is not presented as denial.
- **Day 5 (patience): 1 Corinthians 13:4 or 2 Peter 3:9.** James 5:7-11 was
  chosen because it follows James's denunciation of wage theft (5:1-6).
  Patience is not approval of wrong. It leaves judgment to the Lord. This is
  also the plan's key safety point.
- **Day 6 (kindness): Colossians 3:12-14.** Rejected in favour of Titus 3:1-7,
  which grounds human kindness in God's kindness to the undeserving (3:3-5).
- **Day 8 (faithfulness): Matthew 25:14-30 (talents).** Rejected: the parable
  raises judgment and reward questions that one day cannot handle fairly.
  Lamentations 3:19-26 puts God's faithfulness first and keeps lament honest.
- **Day 10 (self-control): 1 Corinthians 9:24-27 as the primary.** Moved to
  related. Titus 2:11-14 makes grace the trainer, which is the plan's main
  guard against moralism. The athlete picture then shows that effort is real.
- **Proverbs 25:28 / 16:32.** Not used. Proverbs describes wisdom and its
  usual results and is easily turned into moralism. It is better placed in the
  wisdom plan.

## 4. Interpretive issues

- **"Fruit" singular.** Many teachers stress the singular *karpos*. Some
  commentators (e.g. Gordon Fee) treat it as a collective singular rather than
  a doctrine of "one fruit with nine flavours". The text says only what is
  safe on either reading: the nine belong together as the character of one
  Spirit, a portrait of Christ (Galatians 4:19).
- **Flesh/Spirit.** Read as the two ages or two ways of life (Fee,
  Barclay), not as body against soul. Day 1 notes that more than half of the
  "works of the flesh" are relational sins (enmity, strife, jealousy, anger,
  rivalries, dissensions, divisions, envy: 8 of 15 in the usual count). This
  fits the Galatian conflict (5:15).
- **Divine work and human effort.** The Spirit produces the fruit. Believers
  walk by the Spirit, sow to the Spirit and keep in step (5:16, 5:25, 6:8). The
  plan affirms real effort (1 Corinthians 9; Romans 8:13) as a response to
  grace, not a wage (day 10). Wesleyan-Holiness, Reformed and Pentecostal
  readers weight this differently. The wording stays within what all of them
  affirm.
- **Keeping in step (5:25).** *Stoicheō* is often glossed as "walk in line".
  The plan uses "keep in step" without resting on a disputed etymology.
- **Galatians 6:9 harvest.** Paul's encouragement is presented as God's
  timing, never as a promised visible result.
- **Day 9, "His own heart" (Matthew 11:29).** The text says Jesus "describes
  His own heart in terms of gentleness and humility". It does not claim this
  is the only place He does so.
- **French terms.** Segond 21/TOB render the list as amour, joie, paix,
  patience, **bonté** (kindness), **bienveillance** (goodness), foi/fidélité,
  douceur, maîtrise de soi. The FR prose follows this: day 6 uses *bonté*,
  and day 7 speaks of *le bien*. The FR completion uses *fidélité* rather than
  *foi*, for clarity.

## 5. Safety

- **Day 5 (patience)** `safetyNote`: patience never means staying in danger or
  putting up with abuse. It points to a trusted pastor, counsellor or mature
  believer, and to emergency services when danger is immediate.
- **Day 9 (gentleness)** `safetyNote`: gentleness is never silence about harm.
  A child or vulnerable adult at risk → the church's safeguarding lead or the
  authorities. Seeking help and safety is not a failure of gentleness.
- The reflections also guard each quality against its counterfeit (tested):
  - Joy is not forced cheerfulness (day 3).
  - Peace does not deny trouble, grief or disagreement (day 4).
  - Patience never calls wrong right (day 5).
  - Gentleness still confronts (day 9).
  - Self-control is not numbness: Jesus wept and was angry without sin (day 10).
- The self-examination prompts are phrased to avoid shame ("without
  condemning yourself", "gently", "without shame").
- Day 10's practice suggests a small "fast" from a phone, a snack or the last
  word. It avoids food fasting, so readers with health conditions or a history
  of disordered eating are not put at risk.

## 6. Resource topics

`fruit-of-the-spirit` is on every day. The other topics are chosen per day:
- `holy-spirit` on days 1 and 10.
- `character` on days 2, 5 and 8.
- `worship` and `contentment` on day 3 (joy).
- `conflict` on days 4 and 9 (peace, gentleness that restores).
- `boundaries` on day 5, to match its safety point.
- `forgiveness` and `hospitality` on day 6 (Ephesians 4:32; kindness to people
  who cannot repay).
- `generosity` and `holiness` on day 7.
- `trust` on day 8.
- `discipleship` on day 9 (restoring one another).
- `holiness` and `spiritual-rhythms` on day 10.

All ids come from `RESOURCE_TOPICS`, and the shelf stays inside
`christian-living`.

## 7. What reviewers must check

**Theology reviewer**
- Is the fruit presented as the Spirit's work, with effort as a response and
  never as merit? Check days 1 and 10, the intro and the biblical note.
- Is every summary of a passage faithful to its context? Check especially
  James 5 (day 5), Titus 3 (day 6), Nehemiah 8 (day 3) and Lamentations 3
  (day 8).
- Could any paraphrase be read as a quotation? None is intended, but check
  day 6 ("showed His kindness and love by sending the Saviour") and day 10
  ("keep in step with Him").
- Are the Pentecostal/charismatic notes balanced? Day 1 asks to be filled
  afresh. Day 2 addresses those who use spiritual gifts. Day 7 speaks of the
  Spirit's power in Jesus' ministry (Acts 10:38).
- Confirm the three-triad grouping is acceptable when it is labelled as a
  teaching aid.

**Safety reviewer**
- Are the day 5 and day 9 safety notes enough, and do they point to the right
  help in the app's markets? Is "safeguarding lead" understood outside the UK?
- Day 5's prompt 2 ("the person … that most tries your patience") and day 6's
  prompt 3 (praying for someone unkind) must not read as telling anyone to
  endure mistreatment.
- Day 4's `selfPrompt` invites a "peacemaking step" toward an avoided
  disagreement. Is that safe enough without a caveat for abusive relationships?

**Language reviewers**
- FR: tu throughout, idiomatic phrasing. Check "marcher à Son pas" (days 1
  and 10) and the *bonté/bienveillance* choice.
- 14 other languages: day titles and UI keys are AI drafts. The title for day
  6 is rendered as "kindness born of grace" in several languages; check the
  term for *chrestotēs* against each locale's Bible (e.g. es *benignidad* vs
  *amabilidad*, sw *utu wema*).
