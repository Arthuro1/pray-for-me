## 1. Purpose and audience

A gentle, Scripture-centred intercession plan during pregnancy: for the
developing child, the mother, the father or other parent, the wider family, the
people who give care, the home and the child's future, ending by entrusting
everything to God. No `lifeStage` is set: the intro welcomes mothers, fathers,
adoptive or foster parents-to-be, grandparents and friends, and tells a mother
praying it herself to pray the "mother" prompts for herself. Each day: a
reflection on a passage read in context → three prompts → a `selfPrompt` (on
every day) turning the prayer back on the one praying → one small practice.
About ten minutes a day.

The intro says plainly that prayer goes hand in hand with prenatal and medical
care ("keep every appointment"), that the plan promises neither a healthy
pregnancy nor a particular outcome, and that if the pregnancy ends in loss the
reader may pause or stop without it being a failure of faith. The completion
works whether the reader is still waiting, has welcomed a baby, or is grieving
a loss; it never assumes a live birth.

## 3. Passages

**Every passage the spec lists is used**: Psalm 139:13-18 (d1), Psalm 22:9-10
(d19), Psalm 121 (d18), Psalm 127 (d8), Luke 1:39-45 (d4), James 1:5 (d14),
Philippians 4:6-7 (d16), Isaiah 40:11 (d6, related), Jeremiah 1:4-5 (d11),
Isaiah 49:13-16 (d5, covers 15-16), Psalm 71:5-9 (d3, covers 6),
Genesis 1:26-28 (d2), 1 Samuel 1:15-18 (d16, related), Matthew 6:25-34 (d17),
Romans 8:26-28 (d21, related).

Added: Ecclesiastes 11:5 (no one understands how a body is formed in the womb —
humility), Matthew 1:18-25 (Joseph), Proverbs 24:3-4, Ruth 4:13-17 (Naomi and
the community around Obed), 2 Timothy 1:5, Exodus 1:15-21 (Shiphrah and Puah),
Colossians 4:14 (Luke the doctor), 1 Samuel 3:8-10, 2 Timothy 3:14-15,
Deuteronomy 6:4-7, Luke 2:52, Galatians 5:22-23, Ephesians 6:4,
Luke 2:25-38 (Simeon and Anna), Galatians 6:2, Acts 4:32-35, Isaiah 46:3-4,
1 Kings 19:4-8 (Elijah's exhaustion), Mark 6:30-32, Romans 8:31-39.

**Rejected, and why**
- **Exodus 23:26 / Deuteronomy 7:14** ("none will miscarry or be barren") —
  covenant blessings to Israel as a nation, widely quoted as a personal promise
  of no miscarriage; using them would imply that loss means weak faith.
- **Isaiah 66:9** ("do I bring to the moment of birth and not give delivery?") —
  about Zion's restoration; often quoted as a guarantee of safe delivery.
- **John 16:21** (labour pain forgotten for joy) — considered for day 19; it
  assumes a live birth and would wound a bereaved reader. Isaiah 46:3-4 used.
- **1 Timothy 2:15** ("saved through childbearing") — heavily disputed; easily
  heard as blame.
- **3 John 2, Philippians 4:19, Psalm 37:25** — prone to prosperity/health-
  guarantee readings.
- **Genesis 35:16-18** (Rachel dies in childbirth) — too distressing for a daily
  devotional during pregnancy.
- **Psalm 127:3-5** "quiver full" lines are not stressed (d8 focuses on the
  house), to avoid wounding readers with infertility or loss behind them.
- **Luke 1:56** (Mary stays three months with Elizabeth) — considered for d6;
  Isaiah 40:11 fitted the mother's strength better.

## 4. Interpretive issues and how the text handles them

- **Jeremiah 1:5** (d11): said explicitly to be Jeremiah's own prophetic
  calling, "not a promise that every child will be a prophet or have a
  particular destiny". What carries over: God's knowledge precedes our plans;
  we pray for a listening heart (Samuel), not a scripted vocation. Test-guarded.
- **Luke 1** (d4) and **Luke 2** (d15): descriptive, within the unique story of
  Jesus and John — "not a pattern every pregnancy must reproduce"; Simeon and
  Anna show what a praying community can be, not a required experience.
- **Hannah** (d16, related): read for her honest anguish and for leaving the
  temple "still not knowing what would happen" — not as a formula for receiving
  a child. Her vow is not presented as a model.
- **Psalm 121** (d18): a pilgrim song of trust, "not a guarantee that nothing
  hard will ever happen" (other psalms lament troubles that came).
- **Romans 8:28** (d21): read in context — the groaning of vv. 18-27, the
  "good" of v. 29 (conformity to Christ), and the hardships of vv. 35-39 that
  cannot separate God's people from His love. Not "all will end well".
- **Genesis 1:28** (d2): blessing on humanity, "not a fertility guarantee for
  each individual"; a pregnancy is not a measure of worth or favour.
- **Psalm 139 / personhood**: the plan prays for the unborn child as known and
  loved by God, without political or clinical statements.
- **Children who die before birth**: their eternal state is debated among
  Christians; the plan does not address it. Day 21 speaks of *entrusting* the
  child to God, not of a doctrinal claim. A pastor should handle this.
- **Isaiah 40:11**: NIV "those that have young", KJV "those that are with
  young", LSG "les brebis qui allaitent" — applied to pregnancy and nursing.
- **Pentecostal confession practice** ("declare/decree a healthy pregnancy"):
  not used; the test forbids it. Boldness is expressed as asking (d18: "ask
  boldly for protection"), never as claiming an outcome.

## 5. Safety concerns and how the text handles them

Safety notes (only these four days; test-guarded):
- **Day 1** — bleeding, severe pain, reduced baby movements or any worrying
  symptom → midwife or doctor now; emergency services in an emergency.
  "Prayer and medical care belong together."
- **Day 8** — someone at home hurting, threatening or controlling you → tell the
  midwife or doctor in confidence, a domestic abuse helpline, or emergency
  services if in danger. (Placed on the home day rather than the father day, so
  as not to cast suspicion on every father.)
- **Day 16** — past loss, or this pregnancy ending in loss: not your fault, not
  a failure of faith; pastor, trusted believer, bereavement support; midwife or
  doctor can refer.
- **Day 20** — low mood, anxiety or frightening thoughts after a birth *or after
  a loss*: common, treatable, not a failure of faith; tell the midwife/doctor;
  thoughts of harming yourself or your baby → doctor or emergency services
  immediately.

Elsewhere: medical care is affirmed in practical terms (intro; d6 prompt to
report anything that seems wrong "without waiting for certainty"; d10 "keep
every appointment" and a self-prompt against the idea that seeking care is weak
faith; d19 maternity number ready, the birth plan "a list of hopes, not a
test", "no way of giving birth is a spiritual failure"). Days 7 and 8 do not
assume a present or safe father. Days 16, 20 and 21 are written to hold for a
reader after a loss.

## 6. Resource topics

In the `relationships` domain most broad tags pull couples', dating or marriage
books (checked with `resolveResources`, all languages):
- `parenting` → Shepherds Global *Christian Family* (lifeStages single/dating/
  engaged/married; the sw edition is the singleness lesson) and Poonen *A Godly
  Family Life* (marriage); `family` → pre-marriage courses;
  `prayer` → Karambiri *Bien choisir son conjoint*; `trust`, `character`,
  `community`, `church`, `identity`, `friendship` → dating/marriage titles;
  `grief`, `suffering`, `illness` → Ruthe *Charlotte geht* (marriage);
  `abuse-safety` → Karambiri *Les premières années* (marriage).
- So days use `pregnancy` (0 today — the tag exists for this plan's
  researchers), `children` (8 child/parenting titles, incl. Omartian *The Power
  of a Praying Parent*), and tags that pull nothing adult: `intercession`,
  `wisdom`, `scripture-prayer`, `psalms`, `fear`, `lament`, `pastoral-care`.
- Sensitive tags on one day each: `miscarriage` (d16), `mental-health` (d20).
  `illness` is not used (no day names complications in detail).
- Days 16, 20, 21 deliberately carry no `children` tag: parenting books should
  not greet a grieving reader. Their shelves are empty until pregnancy/loss
  resources are catalogued — correct, not a bug.
- One `children` row, Lehmann *Sexualerziehung ist Familiensache* (German,
  children's sex education), is off-topic for pregnancy but not adult material;
  the test allows it.

## 7. What the human reviewers must check

Theology:
- Jeremiah 1:5 (d11), Luke 1–2 (d4, d15), Hannah (d16), Psalm 121 (d18),
  Romans 8:28 (d21) framings.
- Day 20's use of Elijah's exhaustion (1 Kings 19) for the postpartum season —
  apt or too heavy?
- Day 21: "entrusted to the God who gave up His own Son" — acceptable across
  views on children who die before birth?
- No sentence speaks for God; no Scripture quoted (paraphrase only).

Safety (ideally someone with midwifery/perinatal mental-health experience):
- The four safety notes: wording, calm tone, and whether "domestic abuse
  helpline" / "bereavement support" / "emergency services" are right generically
  across countries.
- Day 1 symptom list (bleeding, severe pain, reduced movements) — is it the
  right minimal list, and non-diagnostic?
- Completion and days 16/20/21 read well for a bereaved reader.
- French: tu throughout; "deuil périnatal", "ligne d'aide aux victimes de
  violences conjugales", "la déprime" are natural.

## 8. Outstanding questions

1. **Title**: the spec says "Praying for Your Unborn Child"; since grandparents
   and friends also pray it, "Praying for an Unborn Child" may be more
   inclusive. Kept the spec's title.
2. **Completion screen UI**: if the generic plan-completion screen is
   celebratory (confetti, "Well done"), this plan needs a neutral variant — a
   reader may finish after a loss. Outside this plan's files.
3. **Loss mid-plan**: a way to jump straight to day 21, or to end the plan
   gently, would help a bereaved reader. The intro only promises pause/stop.
4. **Localized helplines**: notes name services generically; a per-country
   helpline list (perinatal loss, perinatal mental health, domestic abuse)
   would be better than any hard-coded number.
5. **Resources wished for**: pregnancy devotionals (e.g. Crossway/B&H "expecting"
   titles), pregnancy-loss resources (Hutto *Inheritance of Tears*, Booker
   *Grace Like Scarlet* — sensitive review), perinatal mental-health guidance
   from a Christian source, French/German/Spanish/Portuguese and African
   pregnancy prayer guides. Consider narrowing the Shepherds Global *Christian
   Family* entry (e.g. per-lesson editions) so `parenting` becomes usable here.

## Resource-topic revision (2026-09-28, integrator)

After the resource research, the day topics were narrowed so each shelf fits
the reader who is likely to be on that day:

- Days 1–10 and 17–19 use `pregnancy` (plus `pastoral-care`, `intercession`,
  `psalms` or `fear` where the day is about that). They no longer carry
  `children`, which pulled the whole children-plan parenting shelf (e.g.
  "letting go of adult children") into pregnancy days.
- Days 11–15 ("The child's future") keep `children`: general books about
  children fit there.
- Days 16, 20 and 21 may be read after a loss, so they carry no `pregnancy`
  and no `children`: day 16 `fear, miscarriage, lament`, day 20
  `mental-health`, day 21 `intercession`. A cheerful pregnancy devotional
  is never what greets a grieving reader.
- Three candidates lost tags that carried them onto the children plan
  (`polimino-praying-through-your-pregnancy`, `brisley-verses-dont-enjoy-motherhood`,
  `tombrella-dads-paternity-leave`).

Both rules are tested: `prayingForUnbornChild.test.js` (shelf anchors) and
`src/content/resources/newPlanResources.test.js` (no plain pregnancy
devotional on days 16, 20, 21).
