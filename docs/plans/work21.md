## 1. Purpose and audience

Brings work under Christ: paid and unpaid work, study, running a household,
caring for others, trades and professions, leading, looking for work,
retirement and ministry. Every day is written to work for every one of those
readers ("your work — paid or unpaid", "your studies", "the work in front of
you"); no prompt assumes a salary, a boss or a career. The plan teaches that
calling is first a calling **to Christ** (day 11), not the search for one
hidden career, and it promises no success, wealth, job or promotion.

Mode: prayer. Category: `formation`. Shelf domain: `christian-living`.

## 3. Passages considered and rejected

Used from the spec list: Genesis 1–3, Proverbs 6:6-11 and 22:29, Ecclesiastes
(2:18-23, 3:9-13, 4:4-8, 9:11), Matthew 6, 20 and 25, Luke 3, Colossians
3:22-4:1 with Ephesians 6:5-9 and James 5, Ephesians 4:28, 1 Thessalonians
4:9-12, James 4:13-17, 1 Timothy 6, Exodus 20 / Deuteronomy 5, Mark 2:27,
Psalm 127:1-2, Daniel 6, Nehemiah 2, Acts 18:1-4, Exodus 31:1-6, 1 Corinthians
7:17-24.

Rejected or deliberately not used:

- **2 Thessalonians 3:6-13** ("unwilling to work … not eat"). It addresses
  believers who *refused* to work while able to, in a setting of idleness
  (possibly linked to end-times expectation). Read out of context it is a club
  against the unemployed, the ill and the disabled. With a day on unemployment
  and a no-shame guardrail, the plan makes the same point positively via
  1 Thessalonians 4:11-12 and Ephesians 4:28 instead. Reviewer may reinstate it
  with careful framing.
- **Deuteronomy 8:18, Malachi 3:10, Luke 6:38, 3 John 2** — standard
  prosperity-teaching proof texts; not needed and too easily read as
  give-to-get.
- **Jeremiah 29:11** — addressed to the exiles as a people; commonly misused as
  "God has one specific plan (career) for you". Kept out to protect the
  no-secret-career guardrail.
- **Luke 5:1-11** (failed night of fishing) — considered for failure; John 21
  was preferred for the final day because it joins failure, restoration and
  the renewed call to follow.
- **Ecclesiastes 5:18-20** — overlaps 3:9-13 (used on day 21).
- **Hebrews 4:9-11** — considered for Sabbath; left out to keep day 20 on the
  commandment itself and Jesus' teaching; a reviewer could add it.

## 4. Interpretive issues and how the text handles them

- **Colossians 3:22 / Ephesians 6:5 (day 3).** Read as words to enslaved people
  in Roman households; the reflection states they do not endorse slavery and
  pairs them with Colossians 4:1 (masters have a Master; give what is just and
  fair), Ephesians 6:9 and James 5:4. The safety note names the texts' misuse.
- **1 Corinthians 7:21 (day 11).** Disputed: "take the opportunity to be free"
  vs "make the most of your present condition". The reflection follows most
  modern translations and says so ("as most translations read it").
- **Calling.** Paul's *klēsis* language is primarily God's call to Christ. The
  plan does not deny particular vocations (Bezalel, day 5; Nehemiah, day 13)
  but refuses the "one hidden career you could miss forever" model; decisions
  are framed as wisdom, counsel and prayer (Nehemiah 2; James 1:5).
- **Proverbs (day 4).** Read as wisdom about how life usually goes, balanced by
  Ecclesiastes 9:11; the text says explicitly that diligence is not a formula.
- **Matthew 6:25-34 (day 7).** Not a promise of wealth nor that believers never
  lack (Philippians 4:12 cited).
- **Matthew 20:1-16 (day 8).** The parable's main point is the landowner's
  (God's) generosity; its use for comparison is primary, the prayer for the
  unhired is a secondary application — reviewer may judge whether that stretch
  is acceptable.
- **Matthew 25:14-30 (day 12).** "Talents" were sums of money; the day notes the
  parable is about readiness for Christ's return, not an investment strategy,
  and applies it to gifts via 1 Peter 4:10.
- **Exodus 31 (day 5).** Bezalel's filling is for a specific task; the day asks
  the reader to pray to be filled with the Spirit for ordinary work (Pentecostal
  vocabulary, in line with Ephesians 5:18) without promising an experience.
- **Psalm 127:2 (day 19).** Translation split: "He grants sleep to those He
  loves" vs "He gives to His beloved *in* sleep". The reflection names both.
- **Sabbath (day 20).** Christians differ (seventh-day observance, Lord's Day,
  principle of rhythm — Romans 14:5-6, Colossians 2:16-17). The day says so,
  gives both rationales (creation, Exodus 20; release from slavery,
  Deuteronomy 5), and frames rest as gift not guilt.
- **James 4:13-17 (day 21).** Not anti-planning; rebukes presumption.
  **John 21:18-19** foretells Peter's death; the day only says he was told
  others would lead him where he did not want to go — it does not generalize
  martyrdom or promise hardship.
- **Ministry vs other work.** Day 11 states neither path is holier; pastors are
  included among those who hold power over others (day 9).

## 5. Safety concerns and how the text handles them

- **Day 3 — forced labour / misuse of slavery texts.** Safety note: these verses
  require neither slavery nor silent endurance; confiscated documents, withheld
  wages, threats or being stopped from leaving = forced labour → police or an
  anti-trafficking helpline.
- **Day 10 — workplace abuse and authority.** Reflection: never obey an order
  that requires sin (Acts 5:29). Safety note: working under authority never
  means enduring abuse or joining wrongdoing; record and report harassment,
  discrimination, threats, unsafe conditions, unpaid wages or pressure to break
  the law (HR, union/staff representative, labour inspectorate, police);
  emergency services if in danger; a pastor or trusted believer can walk with
  you.
- **Day 14 — unemployment.** Not God's disfavour nor a measure of worth; safety
  note on debt, discouragement and despair → pastor/trusted believer, free debt
  advice early, crisis line or emergency services for thoughts of self-harm.
- **Day 19 — exhaustion.** Burnout/sleeplessness/low mood lasting weeks → a
  doctor; needing help is not a lack of faith.
- Without a note but guarded in the prose: day 16 (illness, disability, age as
  limits without shame), day 17 (believers who give out of little must not be
  manipulated into giving), day 18 (anyone who manages others must never use
  their position to press anyone about faith), day 19 (prayer for night
  workers, carers and people holding several jobs who cannot rest as they
  need — no guilt for them).
- Helplines are named generically (no country numbers): Praystead is
  international.
