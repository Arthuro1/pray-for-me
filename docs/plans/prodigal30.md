# prodigal30 — Praying for a Prodigal (30 days)

**Status:** Drafted with AI assistance on 2026-09-23. Not reviewed by a human.
EN and FR authored; the other 14 languages show AI-drafted day titles and fall
back to English prose.

Files: `src/content/plans/prayingForAProdigal.js` (meta),
`prayingForAProdigalDays.js` (days), `prayingForAProdigal.test.js` (contract +
guardrails). UI keys (`planProdigal*`) are in the integrator's scratch JSON
`i18n/prodigal30.json`. Days 1–14 were written in one session and days 15–30
plus the meta, test and note in a second (after a usage-limit interruption);
days 1–14 were re-read and kept as written.

## 2. Purpose and audience

Sustained intercession for someone who once identified with Christian faith or
community and has wandered away: a child, spouse, brother or sister, parent or
friend. It is not the plan for someone who has never believed (that is
`unbelievers30`). The reader prays for the person without treating them as a
project to manage, and every day turns the prayer back on the reader's own heart
(`selfPrompt`). About 10–15 minutes a day. It never promises a return.

## 4. Passages considered and rejected

- **Used from the spec:** every passage in it — Luke 15 (days 1–3, 5, 6, 15),
  Hosea 14, Jeremiah 3:12-14, 22, James 5:19-20, 2 Timothy 2:24-26, Psalm 51,
  Romans 2:4, 1 Corinthians 13, Galatians 6:1-2, 1 Peter 5:7, Luke 22:31-32,
  John 21:15-19 (widened to 21:22 for "what about him? — you follow Me"),
  Psalm 34:18, Isaiah 55:6-11, 2 Peter 3:9, Ezekiel 34:11-16, Matthew 18:12-14.
- **Proverbs 22:6** — rejected as a primary. It is so often read as a guarantee
  that a returning child is "owed" to faithful parents; that reading is the
  opposite of this plan. (Proverbs 22:3, prudence before danger, is used instead.)
- **2 Samuel 18:33 (David and Absalom)** — rejected: the son dies unreconciled;
  too heavy for a daily devotional that many parents of living prodigals will read.
- **1 Peter 3:1-2 (winning a spouse without a word)** — rejected: it addresses
  wives specifically and joins the counsel to submission, which needs careful
  handling around abuse. 1 Peter 3:13-16 carries the "when to speak" point.
- **Genesis 22 / 1 Samuel 1:27-28 as "surrender" texts** — rejected as
  proof-texts; Gethsemane (Mark 14:32-36) models asking and yielding together.
- **Philippians 1:6** — rejected for the final day: it is about believers and
  would read as a promise that God "will finish" the prodigal's return.
- **Matthew 10:16** — considered for the wisdom day, dropped because it could be
  heard as casting the loved one as a wolf.
- **Luke 22:43-44** — avoided (textual variant); Mark's Gethsemane is used.

## 5. Interpretive issues

- **Luke 15 is told to grumblers.** The plan reads the parables in their setting
  (15:1-2): the stories reveal the Father's heart first and expose the
  elder-brother heart second (day 15), rather than being a manual for prodigals.
  The ending is left open, as Jesus leaves it.
- **The father "let him go".** Day 3 reads the father's release as grief that
  refuses to control, not approval or indifference; the father does not chase
  him into the far country. Some readers stress the father's active watching
  (day 6) — both are held.
- **Peter (day 9).** Jesus' knowledge that Peter would turn back is described as
  His knowledge of Peter, "not a formula for every story".
- **Hosea / Jeremiah (days 7, 10).** Covenant words to a nation; the text says
  they do not tell in advance how any one person responds.
- **2 Timothy 2:24-26 (day 12).** "God may grant repentance" is kept as God's
  gift; the "snare" is described as real without speculative demonology or any
  diagnosis of the person. The prompt prays for freedom "whatever its source".
- **Romans 2:4 (day 18).** Often quoted at the wanderer; in context it is
  addressed to the one who judges. The day turns it on the intercessor.
- **Galatians 6:2 and 6:5 (day 26).** The "burden/load" distinction is a common
  but debated application (6:5 is primarily about accountability before God).
  The day uses it modestly, alongside Proverbs 22:3 and Luke 4:28-30.
- **Isaiah 55:11 (day 23).** God's word accomplishes *His* purpose; the text
  explicitly refuses to decide in advance what that work must be.
- **2 Peter 3:9 (day 27).** God's desire that none perish is presented as His
  heart, not a timetable or a statement about any one person's end. Calvinist
  and Arminian readings of "all" differ; the day stays with what both affirm.
- **Revelation 5:8; 8:3-4 (day 28).** Apocalyptic imagery; the text says it is
  picture language and does not tell how or when prayers are answered. Hebrews
  11:13 honestly allows that some pray faithfully and die without seeing it.
- **James 1:6-8 (day 24).** The "doubter" there is the divided, double-minded
  person, not someone with honest questions; the reflection does not use those
  verses against a struggling intercessor.

## 6. Safety concerns

- **No promise of return** anywhere (intro, completion, biblical text and days
  9, 10, 23, 27, 28 say so explicitly). Tested with EN/FR regexes.
- **No claimed knowledge of why someone left.** Day 2 lists several possible
  reasons (doubt, hurt, sin, grief, trauma, mental illness, church failure,
  unanswered questions) and assigns none; day 25 says not every wanderer is a
  rebel.
- **No manipulation.** Day 18 names guilt-trips, ultimatums, ambushes and
  deception as off-limits; days 12, 19, 20, 23 keep speech gentle, invited and
  honest; day 9 limits what is shared about the person.
- **Safety notes:**
  - Day 3 — letting go never means ignoring danger (self-harm/harm to others →
    emergency services or a crisis line).
  - Day 11 — abuse in a church setting: believe them, no pressure to forgive or
    return, police + church safeguarding lead, trained counsellor.
  - Day 26 — boundaries: violence, threats, exploitation, addiction; not a
    failure of love; forgiveness never requires staying in harm's way; pastor,
    counsellor, addiction/family support, police or emergency services,
    protection services for children and vulnerable adults.
- **Grief and lament** are honoured (days 3, 7, 17, 22, 28); self-blame is
  addressed without condemnation (day 17).
- **Family dynamics:** siblings who stayed (day 15), family members blaming each
  other (day 17), decisions about money and events (days 24–26).

## 7. Resource topics

`prodigals` and `intercession` anchor the shelf. `repentance`, `forgiveness`,
`family`, `grief`, `lament`, `trust`, `suffering`, `prayer` follow the spec's
suggested set. Added where a day's subject needs it: `communication`,
`listening`, `apologetics` (days 18–20), `wisdom`, `discernment` (24–25),
`discipleship`, `spiritual-formation` (21), `holy-spirit` (30). Sensitive tags
appear only where the day names the subject and carries a safety note:
`church-hurt` (day 11) and `addiction` (day 26). `boundaries` is on day 26.

## 8. Reviewer checklist

**Theology reviewer**
- Luke 15 readings (days 1–3, 5, 6, 15), especially "the father let him go".
- Day 12: the "snare" and deception language — acceptable for a
  Pentecostal/charismatic audience without implying a spiritual diagnosis?
- Day 18: Romans 2:4 turned on the intercessor.
- Day 26: the Galatians 6:2/6:5 application.
- Days 23, 27, 28: Isaiah 55:11, 2 Peter 3:9 and Revelation 5:8 read without
  promising an outcome.
- French idiom and register (tu; « la personne que tu aimes » + *elle*).

**Safety reviewer**
- Days 3, 11, 26 safety notes: wording, and whether the services named suit
  the app's markets (no numbers are given deliberately).
- Day 16: forgiveness is separated from immediate trust and from staying in harm.
- Day 9 / day 17 practices: sharing details about the person with others.
- Whether any additional day needs a note (e.g. day 25 "when hard times come").

## 9. Outstanding questions

- `Jude 1:24-25` is used on day 30; it passes the reference pipeline test, but
  check it renders well in every locale.
- No `addiction`-only or `mental-health` day exists; mental illness is named
  only as a possible reason on day 2 (no tag), by design.
- The 14 non-EN/FR day titles and the `planProdigal*` UI keys are AI drafts and
  need a native pass; `planProdigalTitle` in de/fr/es/pt/ar etc. avoids the
  gendered "prodigal son" and says "a loved one who has wandered".
- A `deconstruction`/`doubt` resource topic would help this plan and
  `unbelievers30`; today `apologetics` is the nearest.
