# Qetoret — product identity

This is the product's constitution. When a design, copy or engineering decision is
unclear, it is settled here. The technical guardrails (encryption, Scripture
pipeline, plan review) live in `CLAUDE.md` and the docs it links; this file says
**what the product is for**.

> **We do not help people collect prayers. We help them build and tend a life of
> prayer before God.**
>
> From altar to intercession. From intercession to obedience. From obedience to
> mission.

Brand: **Qetoret** · *Let your prayers rise.* · *Build a life of prayer before God.*

The product was called "Pray for Me", then "Praystead". Internal identifiers keep
those names on purpose — see [QETORET_MIGRATION.md](QETORET_MIGRATION.md).

---

## 1. The name

*Qetoret* (קְטֹרֶת) is the Hebrew word for the incense offered before the Lord.
Scripture uses rising incense as a picture associated with the prayers of God's
people:

- Exodus 30:7–8 — incense offered morning and evening, a regular offering
- Psalm 141:2 — prayer set before God like incense
- Revelation 5:8; 8:3–4 — the prayers of the saints rising before God
- Luke 1:5–25 — Zechariah at the altar of incense

The name says: prayer brought intentionally and faithfully before God, in
reverence, for oneself and for others.

**Qetoret does not make prayer acceptable to God and does not carry prayers to
Him.** Access to the Father is through Jesus Christ, the Great High Priest. The
app is a tool that helps believers live intentionally from that access. It is
never a mediator, a sacred object, or a substitute for Christ, the Holy Spirit,
Scripture or the local church.

## 2. Zechariah (Zachariah): the primary product story (Luke 1:5–25)

A priest comes before God during his service. Incense rises. Outside, the people
are praying. At the altar, Zechariah hears that his long-carried prayer has been
heard. The answer, John, prepares the way for the Lord.

Principles drawn from the passage — and how they show up in the product:

| Principle | In the product |
|---|---|
| A believer comes before God | Prayer sessions begin with a quiet transition, not a list |
| Prayer is offered, not just recorded | Every surface leads back to *praying*, not to managing |
| Some prayers are carried for years | "Carried since March 2025" — duration as memory, never leverage |
| The people prayed together | Community is built around carrying, not engagement |
| God acts in His timing | No promised outcomes; surrender and mystery stay |
| A personal answer can serve a Kingdom purpose | Testimony can lead to a faithful next step or encouragement |

The app remembers because people forget. It never implies that God needs
reminders.

## 3. New-covenant identity: a royal priesthood

1 Peter 2:5, 9 · Revelation 1:6; 5:10 · Hebrews 4:14–16; 10:19–22

Believers draw near to God **because of Jesus Christ** — King and Great High
Priest. In Him they belong to a royal priesthood. So prayer flows from identity,
not only from need:

- I have access to God through Christ.
- I can bring my life before God.
- I can carry my household, my people, the Church, those in authority, cities and
  nations before Him.
- I can pray for God's Kingdom and mission.

The posture is **reverent confidence** — bold access through Christ, never
arrogance, entitlement or domination.

### What spiritual authority means here

Authority is **received from** Christ, **exercised under** Christ, shaped by
Scripture, expressed through faith, prayer, obedience, truth, love and service.

Qetoret never teaches that believers control God, dominate people, rule
governments through prayer, decree outcomes into existence, or earn breakthrough
through persistence. *Royal* because we belong to the King. *Priestly* because we
are invited to draw near and intercede. *Missional* because prayer sends us back
into the world in obedience.

## 4. The seven movements

Every significant feature serves at least one.

| Movement | Meaning | Language |
|---|---|---|
| **Come** | Approach God through Christ | *Come before God* |
| **Bring** | Bring what is on the heart | *What would you like to bring before God?* |
| **Carry** | Intercede for others | *Carry this prayer* · *Prayers you're carrying* |
| **Return** | Faithful rhythms | *Prayer rhythm* · *Return to prayer* |
| **Listen** | Scripture, silence, attentiveness | *Remain with God* · *Be still* |
| **Respond** | Prayer leading to obedience | *Is there a faithful next step?* |
| **Remember** | Testimony of God's faithfulness | *Record a testimony* · *Remember God's faithfulness* |

## 5. The seven Intercession Circles

The mature prayer life widens outward. The circles are **reminders of the breadth
of biblical intercession — never levels, scores or achievements.**

| Id | Name | Examples | Posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| `self` | **My heart** | growth, decisions, healing, temptation, provision, calling, repentance, thanksgiving | *Search me. Lead me. Transform me.* |
| `household` | **My house** | spouse, children, parents, siblings, household needs, salvation | *I carry my household before God.* |
| `people` | **My people** | friends, neighbours, colleagues, disciples, those who asked for prayer | *I carry others before God.* |
| `church` | **His Church** | local church, pastors, missionaries, unity, the persecuted | *Strengthen Your Church.* |
| `authorities` | **Authorities** | leaders, judges, lawmakers, public servants (1 Timothy 2:1–2) | *Give our leaders wisdom, justice and restraint.* |
| `nations` | **Nations** | cities, countries, peoples, war, crises, reconciliation | *Your Kingdom come among the nations.* |
| `kingdom` | **Kingdom & Mission** | gospel, discipleship, workers for the harvest, justice, mercy | *Your Kingdom come. Your will be done.* |

Rules:

- A circle is **optional**. Unplaced prayers simply live under *Your prayers*.
- Old prayers are **never** classified automatically — not by AI, not by keyword.
  The user decides ("Where does this prayer belong on your altar?").
- The circle lives **inside the encrypted prayer payload**. It is private
  metadata about prayer content and never becomes a plaintext server column.
- The **Authorities** circle is non-partisan. Qetoret encourages prayer for
  wisdom, justice, peace, integrity and the protection of the vulnerable. It never
  endorses parties or leaders.
- Nation and city choices are always explicit — no geolocation.

## 6. "My Altar" — a metaphor, not a ritual

"Your altar" names the organized expression of a person's ongoing life of prayer.
It is not a replacement for Christ, the church, the Holy Spirit or a biblical
altar, and the UI never renders a literal altar or simulates Temple ritual. It is
expressed through prayer sections, circles, rhythm, stillness and upward motion.

The altar begins small. One prayer is enough. Circles appear as the person's
prayer life grows.

## 7. Product principles

- **Identity before activity.** People pray because Christ has opened the way.
- **Altar before platform.** Qetoret is a place of prayer, not a social network.
- **Priesthood before audience.** No performing spirituality for others.
- **Kingdom before personal empire.** *Your* Kingdom come.
- **Authority with humility.** Boldness with surrender, love and obedience.
- **Intercession before criticism.** See a burden → carry it before God.
- **Prayer before productivity.** Scheduling serves prayer.
- **Faithfulness before streaks.** A missed day produces *Return*, never *Failure*.
- **Scripture before generated spirituality.** Scripture has authority; AI does not.
- **Grace before guilt.** The altar can always be tended again.

### Never build

Leaderboards · spiritual scores · public streaks · "top intercessor" badges ·
trending or "most prayed for" requests · gamified levels · prophecy or "word from
God" generators · guaranteed-breakthrough language · prosperity mechanics ·
partisan content · public prayer profiles by default · automatic AI classification
of private prayers · virtual sacrifices or simulated Temple rituals.

Counts ("12 people are carrying this prayer", "Prayed 38 times") support memory.
They never rank prayers and never measure holiness.

## 8. Theological guardrails

Qetoret does not replace Jesus Christ, mediate access to God, replace the Holy
Spirit, Scripture or the local church, create priestly identity, confer spiritual
authority, guarantee answers, measure spiritual maturity, or speak prophetically
on God's behalf.

**Answered prayer.** The user may testify: *God answered this prayer.* The app
records that testimony. It never declares on its own "God has answered", "God told
you…" or "this proves God's will". It asks: *What happened?* · *How have you seen
God at work?* · *Would you like to record a testimony?* — and says *You marked
this prayer as answered.*

## 9. AI guardrails

AI may help someone find words for prayer, see biblical angles, find Scripture
**references**, organize themes, reflect, and consider faithful actions.

AI must never: impersonate God · claim revelation · prophesy · say "God told me"
or "God is telling you" · guarantee healing or breakthrough · declare a prayer
answered · promise an outcome · invent Scripture · present generated wording as
Bible text · encourage political domination · tell users they control nations
through spiritual authority.

Preferred voice: *"You might pray…"* · *"Scripture invites believers to…"* ·
*"One biblical angle to consider is…"*. Always distinguish biblical teaching,
suggestion and the user's own interpretation. The prompts live only in
`api/anthropic.js`.

## 10. Copy principles

Canonical terms: *Come before God · Bring a prayer · Carry this prayer · Prayer
rhythm · Your altar today · Tend your altar · Return to prayer · Prayers you're
carrying · Remain with God · Record a testimony · Remember God's faithfulness ·
Intercession Circles · My heart · My house · My people · His Church · Authorities ·
Nations · Kingdom & Mission.*

Clarity beats poetry. Not every sentence needs the vocabulary; navigation stays
plain (*Today*, *Journal*), and "Your altar today" lives inside the page.

Avoid language implying magical control, guaranteed outcomes, domination,
superiority over unbelievers, political conquest or spiritual status.

Translation: terms like *altar*, *intercession*, *royal priesthood*, *carry in
prayer*, *Kingdom*, *testimony* and *spiritual authority* are translated for
meaning and denominational neutrality, not word for word — see
`docs/content/CHRISTIAN_TERMINOLOGY.md`. AI-drafted translations stay marked for
native review.

## 11. Motion

One motion idea: **rise**. A short, gentle upward fade when prayer begins, when a
prayer is marked prayed, and when a session ends. No confetti, fireworks or game
rewards. Every animation respects `prefers-reduced-motion` and is never required
to understand state.

## 12. The tests

**The Altar Test.** Does this help the believer build or tend a life of prayer
before God? Does it help them Come, Bring, Carry, Return, Listen, Respond or
Remember — and widen their prayer from self toward Church, authorities, nations
and Kingdom mission? If none apply, question whether it belongs.

**The Royal Priesthood Test.** Does this form consumers or intercessors? Does it
point to Christ's authority or the user's ego? Does it produce service or
entitlement? Does it seek God's Kingdom or a personal empire? Does it encourage
intercession or domination? Does it connect prayer to faithful obedience?

**The Zechariah (Zachariah) Test.** A servant comes before God. Incense rises. A community is
praying. A long-carried prayer has not been forgotten. God acts in His timing. A
private answer participates in a Kingdom purpose. Does Qetoret help users live
inside that kind of prayer story?
